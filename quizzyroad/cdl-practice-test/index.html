CDL knowledge practice tests

To get a commercial driver’s license you pass one or more knowledge tests before the skills test. Which ones you take depends on the license class you want and the endorsements you need. Each state publishes a CDL manual that covers every knowledge test, and our questions are written from that manual with a manual reference for every answer.

Not sure which tests apply to you? Our guide CDL Classes, Endorsements and Restrictions Explained sums up the classes, the endorsements and the knowledge test each one needs.

Passing score: at least 80% correct on each knowledge test, set by federal rule (49 CFR 383.135 (eCFR)). Number of questions: this varies by state, so check the CDL manual from your state’s licensing agency.

General Knowledge

The core CDL knowledge test every applicant takes: vehicle inspection, basic control, seeing, speed and space management, hazards, bad weather, emergencies, accidents and fires, and cargo safety.

Topics: Vehicle inspection, Basic control and shifting, Seeing and communicating, Speed and space management, Hazards and distracted driving, Night, fog and weather, Railroad crossings and mountain driving, Emergencies, ABS and skids, Accidents, fires and alcohol, Cargo safety.

HazMat

For the H endorsement: hazardous materials rules, communication rules, loading and unloading, bulk packaging marking, driving and parking rules, and emergencies.

Topics: Intent of the regulations, Shipper, carrier and driver duties, Communication rules, Loading and unloading, Driving and parking rules, Emergencies.

Air Brakes

Required to drive a commercial vehicle with air brakes without the air-brake restriction: system parts, dual systems, inspecting and using air brakes.

Topics: Air brake system parts, Dual air brake systems, Inspecting air brakes, Using air brakes.

Combination Vehicles

For the Class A license: driving combination vehicles safely, combination air brakes, trailer antilock brakes, coupling and uncoupling, and inspecting a combination vehicle.

Topics: Driving combinations safely, Combination air brakes, Trailer antilock brakes, Coupling and uncoupling, Inspecting a combination vehicle.

Doubles/Triples

For the T endorsement: pulling double and triple trailers, coupling and uncoupling, inspecting doubles and triples, and checking air brakes.

Topics: Pulling double and triple trailers, Coupling and uncoupling, Inspecting doubles and triples, Doubles and triples air brake check.

Passenger

For the P endorsement: vehicle inspection, loading and trip start, driving on the road, after-trip inspection, prohibited practices and brake-door interlocks.

Topics: Vehicle inspection, Loading and trip start, On the road, After-trip vehicle inspection, Prohibited practices, Brake-door interlocks.

School Bus

For the S endorsement: danger zones and mirrors, loading and unloading, emergency exit and evacuation, railroad crossings, student management, antilock brakes and special safety considerations.

Topics: Danger zones and mirrors, Loading and unloading, Emergency exit and evacuation, Railroad-highway crossings, Student management, Antilock brakes, Special safety considerations.

Tank Vehicles

Topics: Tank vehicle endorsement, Inspecting tank vehicles, Driving tank vehicles, Safe driving rules.

Why there is one page per CDL test, not one per state

State CDL manuals are built on the same national model, so the CDL knowledge content is largely the same everywhere. Instead of fifty near-identical state pages, we keep one complete, carefully checked page for each test. If your state’s manual differs on a point, your state’s manual wins.

How to use these practice tests

  • Read the matching section of your state’s CDL manual first.
  • Use practice mode to see the explanation and the manual reference after each question.
  • Switch to exam mode when you can score well above 80% without help.
  • Review every missed question against the manual section we cite.
