        Sample questions

        A few questions from the set. Try to answer before opening each explanation.

        1. Why must air reach all the way to the back of a double or triple rig?

          • A. So the trailer lights get power
          • B. So the landing gear can be raised
          • C. So the dolly ABS lamp will light
          • D. So all the brakes will work
          Show answer

          Answer: D. Air has to flow through every trailer and dolly to the last trailer, or some of the brakes will not work.

          Source: Commercial Driver's License Manual, Version 07/20/2026 (form DMV 60A), section 7.4.1 Additional Air Brake Checks.

        2. In bad weather, why is there a greater chance of skids and loss of traction with double and triple bottoms?

          • A. The rear trailers have no brakes on their axles
          • B. Their tires are narrower than other truck tires
          • C. They have more length and more dead axles to pull
          • D. They have fewer axles than other combination vehicles
          Show answer

          Answer: C. The drive axles have to pull a longer rig with more unpowered axles behind them, so slippery roads and mountain driving call for extra care.

          Source: Commercial Driver's License Manual, Version 07/20/2026 (form DMV 60A), section 7.1.6 Adverse Conditions.

        3. Why should you check that the fifth wheel is not too far forward?

          • A. So the air lines can reach the glad hands without stretching
          • B. So the frame and cab do not hit the landing gear or trailer in turns
          • C. So the trailer does not sit too high on the fifth wheel
          • D. So the locking jaws can reach the head of the kingpin
          Show answer

          Answer: B. If the fifth wheel sits too far forward, parts of the tractor can strike the trailer or its landing gear when the rig turns.

          Source: Commercial Driver's License Manual, Version 07/20/2026 (form DMV 60A), section 7.3.1 Additional Checks.

        4. For a combination whose coupling method is not covered in the manual, how should you learn to couple and uncouple it?

          • A. By using the triples method for every rig
          • B. By using the tractor-semitrailer method
          • C. By asking any driver who has pulled one
          • D. From the manufacturer and/or owner specifications
          Show answer

          Answer: D. There are too many truck-trailer and tractor-trailer types for one manual, so you follow what the maker or owner of your vehicle specifies.

          Source: Commercial Driver's License Manual, Version 07/20/2026 (form DMV 60A), section 7.2.4 Coupling and Uncoupling Other Combinations.

        5. If the tractor protection valve does not work properly and a trailer brake leak drains all the air from the tractor, what could happen?

          • A. The emergency brakes could come on, with possible loss of control
          • B. The trailer lights could stop working until the leak is fixed
          • C. The engine could shut off to save the remaining air
          • D. The service brakes would work but the parking brake would not
          Show answer

          Answer: A. A faulty tractor protection valve lets a trailer leak empty the tractor's air too, and losing that air sets the emergency brakes, which can cost you control.

          Source: Commercial Driver's License Manual, Version 07/20/2026 (form DMV 60A), section 7.4.1 Additional Air Brake Checks.

        Frequently asked questions

        What score do I need to pass?

        Federal rules require at least 80% correct on each CDL knowledge test (49 CFR 383.135 (eCFR)).

        Are these the questions on the real test?

        No. Real test questions are not public. We write our own questions from the official manual, so they cover the same material but are not copies of test questions.

        Is the CDL manual the same in every state?

        State CDL manuals are built on the same national model manual from AAMVA, so the knowledge content is largely the same. We compared 3 state editions for this test: 30 of 30 quotes appear word for word in the same section of all of them. Your own state’s manual is the final word if anything differs.

        How do you check the answers?

        Each question stores the manual section (or page) and an exact quote that a script checks against the manual text, then an independent AI review answers it from that part of the manual alone. See our editorial policy. Found a mistake? Tell us.
